## Mastery

The Night Operator set the bass in the centre as the loudest element, because the washer drum is the clock. The tine keys sit slightly left and the violin slightly right. The theremin is widest, with a short room reverb so its glides hang in the air. The price-gun blips stay dry and about 18 dB under the keys, so they tick rather than snap. There is no bus compression in the B section, so the 5/4 lift keeps its dynamic jump. The empty bar 8 of the Feature is left truly silent, with no tail cut. The coda's room tail decays fully for about three seconds. For rendered audio the target is −16 LUFS integrated, true peak −1.2 dBTP and a loudness range near 12 LU, which the soft coda provides.

## Concept

The direction gave us a room we had never played in: an all-night laundromat on the far side of the Moon. We left the dollar store behind on purpose. Here the machines keep time, which gives our drummerless pulse a reason to exist. Dale's bass is the washer drum, a 7/8 load that lands lopsided on its last three eighths, and Sid's price-gun blips are the coins dropping. The lost socks keep the memories, and that was already Vera's job: the violin carries what the room has forgotten.

This test pressing holds the opening track only, the funny end of the arc we were asked for. The Washer Counts in Seven lets the machine count out loud in clustered keys.
